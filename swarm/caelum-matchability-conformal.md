12. **Paper role:** Background. Basis: open-ended short-answer QA, no misinformation task and no LAYA benchmark; its coverage results cannot sit beside LAYA numbers.
13. **Research question or stated aim:** How to build semantic prediction sets for open-ended LLM generation that separate aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty, and what limits their coverage (§1, Abstract).
14. **Method and design:** Method and evaluation. CAELUM samples answers under an ensemble of clarification prompts, clusters them by semantic equivalence, decomposes uncertainty with kernel-based von Neumann entropy, and calibrates an uncertainty-weighted APS score by split conformal prediction, with the epistemic weight selected on one split and the threshold calibrated on a disjoint split. A matchability result (Theorem 1) bounds population coverage by the fraction of items whose reference answer appears in any sampled cluster; a learned classifier predicts matchability and defines a stratum with its own coverage bound. Main experiments: three generators and six datasets; scale checks with larger generators; four random splits (§1 to §4).
15. **System or model described:** CAELUM, a post-hoc conformal layer over a black-box sampling LLM (4 to 32B generators in the wider experiments). Outputs: a semantic prediction set, uncertainty components U_ale and U_epi, the fitted weight lambda star, and a per-item log ratio R(x). Main runs use 5 clarifications and 50 generated responses per query in the detection experiments (§2, §4).
16. **Dataset(s) used:** Six short-answer QA datasets in the main cells, named in the tables: TriviaQA, AmbigQA, OpenNQ, AmbigInst, SimpleQA, PopQA (§4, Table 1).
17. **Dataset construction and labelling:** Inherited benchmarks. Matchability labels come from a fixed semantic matching rule applied to sampled clusters, with a judge audit that revisits heuristic nonmatches but does not validate all positive matches (§2.1, §5).
18. **Evaluation split:** Disjoint selection split V1 for the weight, calibration split V2 for the threshold, a V0 split (30% of each cell) for the matchability classifier, and a 40% test split; four random splits (§1, §2.4, §4).
19. **Metrics and metric definitions:** Marginal and support-conditional coverage, average prediction set size, AUROC for support-failure and hallucination ranking, abstention rate and precision, and augmented coverage when an abstention label is added to the set (§2, §4).
20. **Threshold, abstention and decision rule:** Prediction set includes clusters whose preceding cumulative mass is at most min(tau_hat times w_lambda(x), 1). Abstention is evaluated as an added label and as a routing trigger, not as part of the headline coverage; the paper states ordinary semantic coverage, augmented coverage, and abstention rate must be reported together (§2.4, §4).
21. **Headline results (as reported):** Coverage is capped by matchability: across 30 LLM and dataset combinations, mean coverage is 0.74 in the learned matchability stratum versus 0.65 overall, averaged over the 78 evaluations using the learned classifier (Abstract; §4). CAELUM and its aleatoric-only (Vanilla) baseline have similar average set sizes (mean about 2.21; mean difference about minus 0.005) (§4 Discussion). The CAELUM detector reaches AUROC 0.780 for support failure, with an abstention rate of 0.459 and augmented coverage 0.924, against 0.696 AUROC and 0.506 abstention for the U_epi trigger (Table 2). Match rates rise only modestly with generator scale, from 61.1% (Qwen3-8B) to 66.0% (Qwen3-32B) on the same questions (§4).
22. **Source of each headline result:** Abstract and §4 for stratum versus overall coverage; Table 1 and Figure 2 for per-cell coverage and matchability; §4 Discussion for set sizes; Table 2 for detection and abstention.
23. **Calibration results and method:** Split conformal calibration with disjoint selection and calibration splits. The central calibration result is negative in a specific way: support-conditional coverage can satisfy its bound while population coverage stays far below the 0.9 target because unmatched items are uncoverable from any subset of sampled clusters (§2.1, §4). Learned-stratum coverage on some factoid datasets, including PopQA, remains below the nominal target (§5).
24. **Abstention and selective prediction results:** See field 21, Table 2 figures. U_epi ranks support failures slightly better than total entropy (higher AUROC in 11 of 17 non-degenerate main cells, average improvement +0.014); R(x) is less consistent and is not a good substitute (§4).
25. **Evidence retrieval: method and results:** No retrieval system is evaluated. The paper's diagnostic reading of a large lambda star is retrieve or abstain under epistemic dominance and clarify under aleatoric dominance, stated as a diagnostic only (§1, Abstract).
26. **Key points relevant to LAYA:** (Shown) A coverage number is capped by support: if the correct answer never appears in the sampled support, no threshold can cover it, so a coverage or calibration claim that does not report its support rate overstates what was measured. This transfers to LAYA's RQ4 as a reporting rule, not a number: any LAYA coverage claim should carry the share of items whose correct option or decisive evidence was reachable in the first place. (Shown) The fitted epistemic weight does not reliably identify an uncertainty regime, because the set-size objective is often flat and several lambda values minimise it (§4, §5); a fitted calibration parameter is not, by itself, evidence about why a model is uncertain. (Inference, labelled) The matchability stratum idea, predicting in advance which items are uncoverable, has a direct LAYA analogue in predicting which validation items have no decisive evidence available before scoring abstention.
27. **Key points non-relevant but notable:** Matchability transfers across generators on the same questions (mean cross-generator AUC 0.78 against 0.80 in-sample), suggesting uncoverability is largely a property of the question, not only of the model (§4). Generating 50 answers per question is the stated cost of the detection setup.
28. **Authors' stated conclusions:** Matchability limits achievable coverage for sampled-support prediction sets; classifiers can predict it and define strata with honest bounds; U_epi ranks support failures better than total entropy on average (§5).
29. **Limitations stated by the authors:** §5: the decomposition need not correspond to ground-truth ambiguity (U_ale AUROC only 0.53 to 0.63 against labelled ambiguity); lambda star need not identify a regime; the judge audit does not validate all positive matches; learned-stratum coverage stays below target on some datasets; transfer evaluations reuse the same questions; 50 generations per question is time-consuming; only short-answer QA is evaluated, not long-form or multi-turn settings; treating limited-support sets as having unconditional nominal coverage may encourage over-trust.
30. **Limitations identified by the extractor:** The headline stratum figure (0.74) mixes learned-classifier rows with oracle-fallback rows in some summaries, and the paper itself flags that including fallbacks raises the figure to 0.83, so the stratum number must be quoted with which rows it includes. No misinformation or multimodal evaluation, so the support ceiling is shown for sampled text answers, not for evidence-based verdicts.
