12. **Paper role:** Background. Basis: no misinformation task and no LAYA benchmark; it studies open re-implementations, not Jev itself and not LAYA.
13. **Research question or stated aim:** Which design choices in open typed-decision models matter: prompt regime (one prompt per question versus all questions jointly), readout complexity, or backbone (§1, Abstract).
14. **Method and design:** Controlled comparison. Five readouts re-implemented and verified against original code, all trained on the same backbone (Qwen3-0.6B), the same 15 public English training sources, the same recipe with a per-head learning-rate sweep, and the same single seed. Evaluation on the 15 in-domain sources and five held-out sources, with paired, row-clustered statistics and a one-point non-inferiority margin. Then the head is fixed and the backbone is varied (scale, base versus post-trained, instruct versus thinking, embedding and reranking checkpoints) (§1, §5, §6).
15. **System or model described:** Typed-decision models answering noul (yes/no), choice, and score questions over a text state, returning a probability distribution per question with no generated text. Readouts: LM letter, learned letter, bilinear-MLP, Clef joint schema head (27.3M new parameters), and the author's minimal cosine head (0.53M) (§2, §3, Table 1).
16. **Dataset(s) used:** 15 public English training sources modelled on Decision 1.0's published mix, plus five held-out sources (including Belebele and a MMLU subset) (§4, §5.3).
17. **Dataset construction and labelling:** Inherited public sources; no new dataset built. Not reported beyond the mix following Decision 1.0's published list.
18. **Evaluation split:** In-domain test portions of the 15 training sources and five held-out sources; held-out sources were not trained on (§1, §5).
19. **Metrics and metric definitions:** Accuracy averaged equally over sources; paired row-clustered bootstrap intervals; McNemar tests for paired readout differences; ECE reported in the bidirectional-attention ablation (§6.1) and appendix E.
20. **Threshold, abstention and decision rule:** No abstention in the evaluation. Decisions are the argmax over each question's option distribution. No abstention in evaluation.
21. **Headline results (as reported):** On Qwen3-0.6B every readout reaches 81.3 to 82.0% in-domain accuracy; the 0.5M cosine head matches the 27M joint schema head in-domain at half its inference time (6.4 ms versus 13.6 ms per row). Joint prompt matches per-question prompting in accuracy, with 1.6 times faster inference and 2.1 to 2.5 times faster training on one H100. Qwen3-0.6B to Qwen3-4B adds 4 points in-domain (82.0 to 85.9) and 11 points held-out (78.0 to 88.9). Base, instruct and thinking checkpoints tie. Embedding and reranking checkpoints bring no in-domain gain and trail by up to 2.1 points held-out (Abstract; §5; §6.2; §6.3; Tables 4 and 7).
22. **Source of each headline result:** Abstract; §5.1 and §5.2 with Table 4 for readout and cost results; §6.2 Table 7 for backbone scaling; §6.3 Tables 8 to 10 for post-training and retrieval-tuned checkpoints.
23. **Calibration results and method:** Limited. Bidirectional-attention variants show higher held-out ECE (0.074 to 0.110) than causal variants (0.068 to 0.071) (§6.1); fuller calibration detail is in appendix E, read in summary only in this pass. No headline calibration claim is made.
24. **Abstention and selective prediction results:** None reported.
25. **Evidence retrieval: method and results:** No retrieval in the systems studied. Retrieval-tuned backbones (Qwen3-Embedding, Qwen3-Reranker) were tested as backbones and did not help (§6.3).
26. **Key points relevant to LAYA:** (Shown) Under one controlled protocol, readout complexity did not move accuracy and a joint prompt matched per-question prompting, while backbone scale did move accuracy, especially held-out (+11 points). This bears directly on RQ1: for typed-decision systems, effort belongs on the backbone and the data, not on head complexity. (Shown) The LM letter readout's one held-out lead came entirely from Belebele and collapsed when its answer codes were swapped at evaluation (76.1% with uppercase letters to 59.3% with symbols), a warning that a readout can borrow the language model prior rather than measure the state (§5.3). (Inference, labelled) These results are on text-only English judgment sources with one seed; they do not measure Jev, LAYA's model, or any misinformation task, so they set a prior, not a verdict, for LAYA design choices.
27. **Key points non-relevant but notable:** Training stability was fragile at scale: the default LoRA learning rate diverged on four larger runs and each table marks the lowered rates used (§6.4), a concrete reproducibility cost in this model family.
28. **Authors' stated conclusions:** Spend effort on the backbone and keep the head minimal; one prompt per row is enough (§7).
29. **Limitations stated by the authors:** The small in-domain pooling differences do not establish statistical equivalence (§6.1); the bidirectional-attention experiments do not isolate the cause of the deficit (§6.1); the 8B retrieval-tuning gap on one source may reflect a single high reference run (§6.3).
30. **Limitations identified by the extractor:** A single training seed throughout, so seed variance is unmeasured in a study whose headline gaps are under one point between readouts. All five readouts are the author's re-implementations, not the original systems. No misinformation, multimodal, or abstention evaluation, so nothing here is a LAYA comparator.
31. **Funding and conflicts of interest:** Not reported in the text read.
32. **Code, data and materials availability:** Code stated to be open sourced at github.com/Ritvik19/DecideAnything (§1); training sources are public.
33. **Risk of bias and quality appraisal, by domain:** (a) Selection of the reported result — Low concern: the comparison protocol, margin, and paired statistics are stated in advance of the results sections, and negative results are reported. (b) Missing data and dropped items — Some concerns: no abstention, but diverged training runs were rerun at lowered rates and are marked rather than counted in any failure tally. (c) Measurement of the outcome — Low concern: accuracy is defined per source with paired intervals. (d) Leakage and contamination — Some concerns: held-out sources are public and may appear in backbone pretraining; this is not addressed in the text read. (e) Deviations from the stated method — Some concerns: per-run learning-rate changes after divergence are disclosed and marked, but they break the single-recipe claim for the affected runs.
34. **Overall quality judgement:** Some concerns. The judgement supports using the paper as a controlled-design prior for RQ1; it does not support treating any of its numbers as comparable to LAYA numbers.
35. **Comparability verdict:** No, not comparable
36. **Comparability scoping note:** No dataset, split, metric, or decision rule is shared with any LAYA result: the paper evaluates text-only typed judgments on its own 15 plus 5 sources, with no abstention and no misinformation task. Its numbers must not sit beside LAYA numbers.
37. **Discrepancies and unresolved disagreements:** Board pass (chair, 2026-11-27): proponent case (a rare fully controlled comparison in the System One family, directly relevant to LAYA's batched typed-decision practice and to the prior run's bundling avenue) against the skeptic case (single seed, re-implementations only, no Jev, no misinformation task, joint versus per-question tie measured on a different model class). Outcome: Background only. One scoped consequence is recorded: Rastogi's joint-prompt tie weakens any expectation of a large bundling effect in general, but does not settle LAYA's own bundling question, which stays with the proposed bundling sensitivity audit.
